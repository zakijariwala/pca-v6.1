# B23 drills

Quick trap checks drawn from the B23 top-30 list.

### B23-D001 | pillars: cost
Scenario: A sandbox budget is set at USD 50. A teammate says spending will stop at USD 50. What's true?
A) Spending stops at the budget.
B) Budgets alert; they don't cap spend.
C) Budgets cap spend only for Compute Engine.
D) Budgets cap spend after 24 hours.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
B. Why: capping needs Pub/Sub notifications and automation.
A fails: budgets don't stop spend.
C fails: no service is capped.
D fails: no delayed cap.
Verified: 2026-09-24
</details>

### B23-D002 | pillars: reliability
Scenario: Network A peers with B, and B peers with C. Can A reach C through B?
A) Yes, peering is transitive.
B) Yes, if B enables routing.
C) No, peering isn't transitive.
D) Only in the same region.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
C. Why: A and C need their own connection or a hub such as Network Connectivity Center.
A fails: it isn't transitive.
B fails: no setting makes it transitive.
D fails: region isn't the reason.
Verified: 2026-09-24
</details>

### B23-D003 | pillars: reliability
Scenario: Which option alone gives Cloud SQL automatic failover in a zone outage?
A) A read replica in another zone.
B) Daily backups.
C) The HA (regional) configuration.
D) A larger machine type.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
C. Why: HA runs a standby in a second zone and fails over to it.
A fails: replicas don't fail over on their own.
B fails: restores, not failover.
D fails: size doesn't add a zone.
Verified: 2026-09-24
</details>

### B23-D006 | pillars: cost
Scenario: Data is read every week. Someone moves it to Archive class "because it's cheapest." What happens?
A) Costs fall.
B) Reads become slow.
C) The data becomes read-only.
D) Retrieval fees and the 365-day minimum can make it cost more than a warmer class.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
D. Why: Archive suits data read less than once a year.
A fails: frequent reads add fees.
B fails: access stays in milliseconds.
C fails: objects aren't made read-only.
Verified: 2026-09-24
</details>

### B23-D008 | pillars: security
Scenario: A chatbot's defense against prompt injection is a system prompt saying "ignore malicious instructions." What should be added?
A) A longer system prompt.
B) Model Armor screening prompts and responses.
C) A bigger model.
D) Cloud Armor.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
B. Why: injection targets the prompt itself; screening happens outside the model.
A fails: still bypassable.
C fails: size isn't a defense.
D fails: HTTP-level protection, not LLM content.

</details>

### B23-D009 | pillars: reliability, cost
Scenario: An internal tool has a one-day RTO. Someone proposes hot DR across two regions. What's the better fit?
A) Hot DR as proposed.
B) No DR.
C) Three regions, active-active.
D) Cold or warm DR, sized to the one-day RTO and the RPO.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
D. Why: match the pattern to the targets; hot DR pays for speed nobody needs.
A fails: over-built.
B fails: meets no target.
C fails: more over-built.
Verified: 2026-09-24
</details>
