### B01-D001 | pillars: security, operational-excellence
Scenario: A company has three business units, each with dev and prod. The security team sets different policies for prod than for dev across all units. Which folder layout makes policy simplest?
A) Every project placed under the organization, with no folders.
B) Organization → prod and dev folders → business-unit folders → projects.
C) One project per business unit holding both environments.
D) Separate organizations per environment.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
B. Why: put the axis where policies differ most at the top, so each policy attaches once and inherits down.
A fails: every policy needs per-project setup.
C fails: mixes environments in one project.
D fails: splits identity and governance for no need.
Verified: 2026-09-24
</details>

### B01-D002 | pillars: security
Scenario: A user holds Viewer on folder `retail`. A project owner under `retail` wants to remove that access for their project only. What can they do?
A) Remove the grant at the project level.
B) Nothing at the project level removes an inherited allow grant; change the folder grant or use a deny policy.
C) Move the user to a different group.
D) Delete the folder.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
B. Why: allow policies inherit down the hierarchy and a lower level can't revoke them.
A fails: the grant isn't set at the project.
C fails: the grant may be direct, and moving groups affects other access.
D fails: destroys far more than one grant.
Verified: 2026-09-24
</details>

### B01-D003 | pillars: cost
Scenario: A sandbox project must stop costing money once it reaches USD 100 this month. What meets this?
A) A budget at USD 100 with a 100% threshold.
B) A quota on Compute Engine CPUs.
C) Labels on every resource.
D) A budget whose notifications go to Pub/Sub, triggering code that disables billing on the project.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
D. Why: budgets only alert; stopping spend needs automation on the notification.
A fails: sends an alert, spending continues.
B fails: caps one resource type, not cost.
C fails: attribution, not control.

</details>

### B01-D010 | pillars: security, operational-excellence
Scenario: A company is starting its Google Cloud adoption and wants consistent governance before teams build. What should come first?
A) Let each team create projects and fix governance later.
B) Buy committed use discounts.
C) A landing zone: resource hierarchy, identity, networking, security guardrails, and logging, deployed as code.
D) Migrate the biggest application first.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
C. Why: the landing zone is the baseline every workload lands on; retrofitting it later costs more.
A fails: governance becomes a clean-up project.
B fails: commits spend before needs are known.
D fails: builds on no foundation.

</details>
