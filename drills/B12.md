### B12-D001 | pillars: reliability, cost
Scenario: An internal tool has an RTO of 24 hours and an RPO of 24 hours. The business wants the cheapest DR that meets them. Which pattern?
A) Hot.
B) Warm.
C) Cold: backups restored into a new environment on disaster.
D) Active-active in three regions.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
C. Why: generous targets fit the cheapest pattern.
A fails: full standby capacity isn't needed.
B fails: pays for running standby resources.
D fails: far beyond the targets.

### B12-D002 | pillars: reliability, cost
Scenario: A service needs an RTO of 2 hours and an RPO of 5 minutes, at moderate cost. Which pattern?
A) Cold.
B) Warm: data replicates without pause and a scaled-down environment scales up on disaster.
C) Hot with full duplicate capacity.
D) No DR plan.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
B. Why: continuous replication meets the RPO; a scaled-down standby meets the RTO at less cost than hot.
A fails: restoring from backups can't meet a 5-minute RPO.
C fails: costs more than needed.
D fails: meets nothing.
Verified: 2026-09-24
</details>

### B12-D003 | pillars: reliability
Scenario: An SLO says 99.9% of requests succeed over 30 days. What's the error budget?
A) 0.1% of requests in the 30-day window.
B) 99.9% of requests.
C) 1% of requests.
D) Zero failures.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
A. Why: the error budget is 1 minus the SLO.
B fails: that's the target.
C fails: that would be a 99% SLO.
D fails: that's a 100% SLO.

### B12-D010 | pillars: reliability, cost
Scenario: A workload has a 99.5% availability target. An architect proposes active-active across two regions. What's the problem?
A) Nothing; more is always better.
B) Two regions can't run active-active.
C) Load balancers can't span regions.
D) The design costs far more than the target requires; zonal redundancy in one region may meet it.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
D. Why: match the failure domain to the target; multi-region adds cost and complexity.
A fails: over-engineering has a cost.
B fails: it's possible, just not needed.
C fails: global load balancers span regions.

</details>
