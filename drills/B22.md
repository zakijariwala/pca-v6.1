# B22 drills

Questions about the remediation method itself.

### B22-D001 | pillars: operational-excellence
Scenario: A learner misses a question on Cloud SQL replicas. What's the first remediation step?
A) Reread every database block.
B) Write the four lines: what you said, what's true, the root cause, and a retest idea.
C) Memorize the missed question's answer.
D) Move on; one miss doesn't matter.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
B. Why: pinning the exact wrong belief makes the fix targeted.
A fails: broad rereading misses the specific misconception.
C fails: the next question uses different words.
D fails: misses repeat until fixed.
Verified: 2026-09-24
</details>

### B22-D002 | pillars: operational-excellence
Scenario: Three missed questions all involved choosing a bigger, more powerful service than needed. What does that suggest?
A) Three separate weak topics.
B) Bad luck.
C) One shared root cause, such as defaulting to the most capable option, to fix once.
D) The questions were wrong.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
C. Why: grouping root causes shows patterns worth one fix.
A fails: misses the common thread.
B fails: patterns aren't luck.
D fails: blames the test instead of learning.

</details>

### B22-D004 | pillars: operational-excellence
Scenario: When is a weak spot closed under the B22 method?
A) After rereading the lesson once.
B) When 7 of 8 retest questions are correct, answered cold at least a day after writing them.
C) When the learner feels confident.
D) After one correct retest answer.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
B. Why: a cold, repeated test shows the misconception is gone.
A fails: reading isn't proof.
C fails: confidence isn't evidence.
D fails: one answer can be luck.

</details>

### B22-D005 | pillars: operational-excellence
Scenario: A learner writes retest questions whose wording hints at the answer. What's the problem, and the fix?
A) No problem; easier questions build confidence.
B) Make every question longer.
C) Use the original missed question again.
D) The retest proves nothing; vary context and wording, and put the tempting wrong answer in at least half of them.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
D. Why: retests must make the old mistake tempting to show it's gone.
A fails: false confidence.
B fails: length isn't the issue.
C fails: memory of the answer, not understanding.
Verified: 2026-09-24
</details>
